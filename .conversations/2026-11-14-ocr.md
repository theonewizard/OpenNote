## Rulings

- R-P6-018: server-side OCR is Tesseract from EPEL 10, installed in the `opennote-api` image. The worker runs from that image too, so there is no separate worker image. A separate image would add a second image to releases, Helm and the Kubernetes manifests (ADR-0010 amendment). The EPEL 10 signing key is committed (fingerprint `7D8D15CBFC4E62688591FB2633D98517E37ED158`, from Fedora's distribution-gpg-keys), and `epel-release` is installed only after its signature checks out against that key. The image carries English (`tesseract-langpack-eng`); other languages are an operator's derived image plus `OPENNOTE_OCR_LANGUAGES`.
- R-P6-019: PDFs are read with PDFium through `pypdfium2`, a new dependency: a self-contained wheel, BSD-3-Clause and Apache-2.0, released 2026-11-09. A page with a text layer gives that text. A page without one is rendered at 300 dpi (capped at 25 megapixels) to a greyscale PGM and read by Tesseract. Tesseract cannot read PDFs, and poppler is not in the UBI repositories, so this is the smallest working path. Images go to Tesseract unchanged; it reads PNG, JPEG, TIFF, GIF, WebP and BMP itself. At most 50 pages per PDF are read, and at most 1,000,000 characters are kept per attachment.
- R-P6-020: OCR follows the AI embedding job's pattern. A commit of an image or PDF attachment through the API enqueues `ocr_vault_job` for the vault, and a run reads every live attachment whose (vault, content hash) has no `ocr_texts` row. Each file is read in a child process (`python -m app.ocr`), limited to 2 GiB of address space (RLIMIT_AS, inherited by Tesseract) and 10 minutes. This keeps PDFium and Tesseract out of the worker's own memory. Unreadable files get a `failed` row and are not retried. Rows whose hash no live attachment in the vault has any more are deleted on the next run, so deleted scans do not stay searchable. A run reads nothing while Tesseract is missing or a configured language has no data, so no file is wrongly marked as failed. `python -m app.ocr.vault <vault-id>` reads a vault on demand.
- R-P6-021: search words and phrases also match `ocr_texts.tsv` (`simple` configuration, GIN index) of the live attachments a note links to or embeds. The note is the hit, not the attachment, and its snippet still comes from the note body. Trigram partial-word matching does not extend to OCR text.
